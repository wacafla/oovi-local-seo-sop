Backlinks from relevant local and industry websites increase a client's domain authority and reinforce their local presence. Unlike national link building, local link building focuses on geographic and industry relevance over raw domain authority.

## What Makes a Good Local Link

Not all backlinks are created equal. For local SEO, a link's relevance to the client's geography and industry matters far more than its raw Domain Authority (DA) score. The best local links come from:

- **Local news outlets and online publications** — A mention in the local newspaper or city blog carries significant trust and geographic relevance.
- **Chambers of Commerce and business associations** — These are high-trust, geographically relevant, and often easy to obtain by becoming a member.
- **Industry associations and directories** — Links from recognized trade organizations validate the client's expertise in their field.
- **Community sponsorships** — Sponsoring a local event, sports team, nonprofit, or school program often earns a permanent, contextual backlink from an organization with deep local ties.
- **Local bloggers and community websites** — A featured article on a neighborhood blog may have a low DA but can send powerful local relevance signals.

A link from the local newspaper or city Chamber is worth more in local SEO terms than a generic DA20 guest post on an unrelated national blog. Always prioritize relevance over metrics.

## Link Prospecting

Prospecting is the process of identifying websites that are good candidates for a backlink. Build the prospecting list before writing a single outreach email.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Analyze Competitor Backlink Profiles">
    Open Ahrefs or SEMrush and run a backlink analysis on the top 3–5 local competitors (businesses ranking above the client in the Local Pack or organic results). Export their backlink profiles and filter for local and industry-relevant sources. Any site linking to multiple competitors but not the client is a high-priority prospect.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use Targeted Google Search Queries">
    Run the following search patterns to uncover additional opportunities:
    - `[city] + "guest post"` or `[city] + "write for us"`
    - `[city] + business directory`
    - `[industry] + [city] + blog`
    - `[city] + chamber of commerce`
    - `[city] + annual event` or `[city] + nonprofit sponsorship`

    Review the first 2–3 pages of results for each query and add relevant sites to the prospecting list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify Local Sponsorship Opportunities">
    Search for local events, nonprofit organizations, school sports teams, community groups, and local podcasts that accept sponsorships. Many of these organizations list sponsors on their websites with a link. Sponsorships are among the easiest local links to earn and are often renewable year over year.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find Unlinked Brand Mentions">
    Set up a Google Alert for the client's business name. Use Ahrefs Alerts or SEMrush Brand Monitoring to find existing web mentions that don't include a link to the client's website. These are warm outreach targets — the author already knows the business, which makes conversion significantly easier.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log All Prospects in the Tracker">
    Record every prospect in the client's Link Prospecting Tracker (Google Sheets). Include the following columns for each entry: **Website URL**, **Domain Authority**, **Contact Name** (if known), **Contact Email**, **Link Opportunity Type** (guest post, sponsorship, directory, unlinked mention, etc.), and **Outreach Status** (not started, contacted, followed up, won, declined). Keep this tracker up to date — it's the single source of truth for link building progress.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Outreach Process

Once the prospecting list is built, begin outreach in batches of 10–15 contacts per week. Quality of personalization matters more than volume of emails sent.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Personalize Every Email">
    Reference something specific about the target website in your opening line — a recent article they published, a specific page you found useful, or something you genuinely appreciate about their work. Generic emails get ignored. Personalization shows you've done your homework and increases response rates significantly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lead With Value">
    Don't open with what you want — open with what you're offering. Frame the outreach around the value to them: a well-researched guest post their audience will appreciate, a resource that complements their existing content, a testimonial they can use on their site, or a sponsorship that aligns with their mission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use Oovi Templates as a Starting Point">
    The Oovi Outreach Email Templates are available in the shared Google Drive under `/Resources/Templates/Link Building/`. Use them as a structural guide — never copy-paste without personalizing. Templated emails that feel automated will hurt your response rate and can damage the client's reputation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow Up Once">
    If you receive no response after 5–7 business days, send a single brief follow-up email. Keep it short: reference your original email and ask if they had a chance to review it. Do not send more than one follow-up per prospect. Persistent follow-up after that crosses into spam territory.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log All Outreach Activity">
    Update the Link Prospecting Tracker after every action — initial email sent, follow-up sent, response received, outcome determined. Accurate tracking ensures nothing falls through the cracks and gives the Account Manager visibility into campaign progress during client calls.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tracking Placements

When a link goes live, the work isn't finished — you need to verify and document the placement.

Once a contact confirms a link is published, navigate to the live page and confirm the following:

1. The link is present and points to the correct URL on the client's website.
2. The link is **dofollow** (check by inspecting the anchor element — a `rel="nofollow"` or `rel="sponsored"` attribute means it won't pass link equity).
3. The anchor text is relevant and natural.

Verify the link has been indexed by Ahrefs within 2 weeks of going live. Log the following in the **Link Report** tab of the client's campaign tracker: the live page URL, the target URL, anchor text, Domain Authority, link type (dofollow/nofollow), and the date it was confirmed live.

Report all new placements to the Account Manager by the 5th of each month so they can be included in the client's monthly reporting.

<Warning>
  Never buy links, participate in link exchange schemes, or use Private Blog Networks (PBNs). These tactics are explicit violations of Google's Webmaster Guidelines. A manual penalty resulting from paid or manipulative links can suppress a client's rankings for months or permanently — and recovery requires a link audit, disavow file submission, and manual reconsideration request that can take 6–12 months to resolve. No shortcut is worth the risk to the client's business.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Target 2–4 quality local links per month per client. This may feel slow, but consistent monthly link acquisition compounds over time and builds a natural, sustainable backlink profile. Irregular bursts of links — especially in the early stages of a campaign — can trigger algorithmic scrutiny. Steady, patient outreach is the right approach.
</Note>
