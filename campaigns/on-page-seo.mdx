On-page SEO ensures the client's website reinforces the local signals from their GBP and citations. Even a well-optimized GBP will underperform if the website doesn't support it with relevant, location-specific content.

## Priority Pages

Start every on-page engagement with the homepage and the client's top service pages. These pages drive the most traffic and carry the most authority, so improvements here deliver the greatest ranking impact in the shortest time.

As a general rule, each distinct service the client offers should have its own dedicated page. A plumber offering "drain cleaning," "water heater installation," and "pipe repair" should have three separate service pages — not a single "Services" page listing all three. Dedicated pages allow you to target specific keywords and locations more precisely, and they give Google a clearer picture of what the business offers.

## On-Page Optimization Checklist

Work through these steps for the homepage first, then repeat for each priority service page. Check off each item in the client's On-Page Audit tracker in Google Drive as you complete it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Title Tags">
    Write a unique title tag for each page in this format: `[Primary Service] in [City] | [Business Name]`. Keep it under 60 characters to prevent truncation in search results. Include the primary keyword and city naturally — do not repeat them or stuff multiple locations into a single tag.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Meta Descriptions">
    Write a unique meta description for each page between 150–160 characters. Include the primary keyword, the city, and a clear call-to-action (e.g., "Call today for a free estimate" or "Book online in minutes"). Meta descriptions don't directly influence rankings, but they drive click-through rates — write them for humans, not algorithms.
  </Step>
  <Step title="H1 Tag">
    Every page must have exactly one H1 tag. It must include the primary keyword and the location. The H1 should match or closely mirror the title tag while reading naturally to a human visitor. Never leave the H1 as a generic default like "Home" or "Welcome."
  </Step>
  <Step title="H2 and H3 Structure">
    Use H2 and H3 subheadings to organize page content into logical sections. Work secondary keywords and nearby cities or neighborhoods into subheadings where they fit naturally. Avoid forcing keyword variations into headings that don't make sense — readability always comes first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Body Content">
    Write a minimum of 500 words per service page. Each page must cover: what the service is, who it's for, why the client is the right choice, the geographic service area, and a clear call-to-action. Thin content (fewer than 300 words) signals low value to Google and often underperforms even with perfect technical optimization.
  </Step>
  <Step title="NAP on the Website">
    Confirm the business Name, Address, and Phone number appear in the website footer on every page. The NAP must also appear on the Contact page. Most importantly, it must be **character-for-character identical** to the NAP on GBP and all citations. Even a minor formatting difference can create a signal mismatch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Local Schema Markup">
    Implement `LocalBusiness` schema (or the appropriate subtype, e.g., `Plumber`, `MedicalBusiness`) on the homepage. At minimum, include the following properties: `name`, `address`, `telephone`, `url`, `openingHours`, `geo` (latitude and longitude), and `areaServed`. Validate the markup using Google's Rich Results Test before deploying. Document the schema implementation in the client's technical notes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Internal Linking">
    Link between service pages and from the homepage to each primary service page. Use descriptive, keyword-rich anchor text (e.g., "our drain cleaning services in Austin") rather than generic phrases like "click here" or "learn more." A clear internal linking structure helps Google understand site hierarchy and distributes page authority across the site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Page Speed">
    Run the page through Google PageSpeed Insights and review both the mobile and desktop scores. Address any issues flagged as **Critical** or **High Impact** before moving on. Target a minimum score of 70 on mobile. Common quick wins include compressing images, enabling browser caching, and removing render-blocking scripts. Log the baseline score and re-test after changes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mobile-Friendliness">
    Test each optimized page using Google's Mobile-Friendly Test tool. All pages must pass with no usability issues flagged. Pay particular attention to tap target sizes, font readability, and viewport configuration. A page that fails mobile-friendliness testing is effectively penalized in mobile search results.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Location Pages

For clients serving multiple cities or operating across multiple locations, create dedicated location-specific landing pages. These pages allow you to target geo-modified keywords for each service area without diluting the authority of the main service pages.

Each location page must have genuinely unique content. Do not simply copy a service page and swap the city name — Google identifies and discounts duplicate content, and users can tell. Unique elements to include on each location page:

- A reference to a local landmark, neighborhood, or community detail
- Location-specific testimonials or case studies (if available)
- A map embed showing the service area
- Unique body copy that speaks to the specific city or region

Location pages perform best when they're well-linked from the homepage, the main navigation, or a dedicated "Areas We Serve" hub page.
