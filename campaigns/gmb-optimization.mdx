Google Business Profile (GBP) is the single most important asset in a local SEO campaign. Optimizing it fully and maintaining it consistently drives rankings in the Local Pack and Google Maps.

## Why GBP Matters

GBP directly influences Local Pack rankings through three core factors: **relevance** (categories and keywords), **prominence** (reviews and citations), and **proximity** (how close the business is to the searcher). Google uses the information on a profile — categories, business description, photos, and activity signals — to determine which businesses to surface for a given query. A fully optimized and actively maintained profile can capture 50%+ of local search clicks, making it the highest-leverage activity in any local SEO campaign.

## Initial Optimization Checklist

Work through these steps in order when onboarding a new client or taking over an existing profile. Do not skip steps, even if parts of the profile appear to already be filled in — verify every field.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Claim and Verify the Listing">
    If the profile is not already verified, initiate verification immediately. Google offers several verification methods (postcard, phone, video, or instant). Postcard verification typically takes 1–14 days. Video verification is faster when available. Document the verification method used and the expected completion date in the client's project notes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the Exact Legal Business Name">
    Enter the business name exactly as it appears on the storefront, official documents, and other listings. Do not add city names, keywords, or taglines to the name field. Inconsistency here creates citation conflicts — and keyword stuffing violates Google's guidelines.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the Primary Business Category">
    The primary category is the single most important ranking signal on GBP. Choose the most specific category that accurately describes the business's core offering. When in doubt, search how top-ranking local competitors categorize themselves and use that as a benchmark.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add All Relevant Secondary Categories">
    Add up to 9 secondary categories that reflect the full range of services offered. Secondary categories broaden the search queries the profile can rank for. Only include categories the business genuinely offers — irrelevant categories do not help and may confuse Google's relevance signals.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a Keyword-Rich Business Description">
    Write a compelling description up to 750 characters. Include the primary city, the main service or offering, and a clear call-to-action. Weave in relevant keywords naturally — do not list keywords. The description does not directly influence ranking, but it reinforces relevance and influences conversions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Address or Service Area">
    For brick-and-mortar businesses, enter the complete, verified street address. For Service Area Businesses (SABs) — businesses that travel to customers — hide the address and configure service areas by city, ZIP code, or region. Never display a P.O. Box or virtual office address as a physical location.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Accurate Business Hours">
    Enter all regular hours and configure special or holiday hours in advance. Inaccurate hours lead to negative reviews and reduce consumer trust. Update hours proactively before public holidays rather than reactively after complaints.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add All Contact Information">
    Enter the primary phone number, any additional phone numbers, the website URL, and an appointment booking link if the client uses one. Verify the phone number matches the number on the website and all citations exactly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload a Minimum of 10 Photos">
    Upload at least 10 high-quality photos across the following categories: exterior (storefront/building), interior (workspace or waiting area), team members, products or services in action, and the business logo. Photos increase engagement and give Google more signals about the nature of the business. Aim for a minimum resolution of 720×540px.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Services and Products">
    Use the Services and/or Products sections to list every offering with a name, description, and price (where applicable). This content feeds directly into GBP search features and can trigger rich results in the Local Pack. Be thorough — this section is frequently left incomplete.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable Messaging (Conditional)">
    Enable the messaging feature only if the client has committed to responding within 24 hours. Google can disable messaging on profiles that consistently fail to respond. If the client cannot meet this requirement, leave messaging off for now and revisit during the next check-in.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Ongoing Monthly Tasks

After the initial optimization is complete, GBP requires consistent monthly maintenance to stay competitive. Add these tasks to the client's monthly workflow:

- Publish **2–4 GBP posts per month** using What's New, Offer, or Event post types. Posts keep the profile active and can highlight seasonal promotions or new services.
- **Respond to all new reviews within 24–48 hours.** Refer to the Review Management SOP for response guidelines and templates.
- **Monitor and answer the Q&A section.** Proactively add your own questions and detailed answers to address common customer queries before they're asked. This also reduces the chance of inaccurate public-submitted answers going unnoticed.
- **Check for suggested edits from the public.** Google allows anyone to suggest changes to a listing. Review these regularly and approve or reject them as appropriate. Unaddressed edits can auto-apply after a period of time.
- **Update seasonal or holiday hours** in advance to prevent Google from marking the profile as having incorrect hours.

## GBP Post Best Practices

Regular posting signals to Google that the profile is actively managed, and it gives potential customers timely reasons to engage.

<Tip>
  Posts expire after 7 days unless they're Event or Offer posts, which remain active until their end date. Write all posts in second person and always include a call-to-action button (e.g., "Book Now," "Learn More," "Call Today"). Use a high-quality image sized at a minimum of 720×540px — blurry or low-resolution images are automatically cropped or rejected by Google.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Never keyword-stuff the business name field. Google can and does suspend listings for this practice — and reinstatement is a slow, frustrating process. The name entered in GBP must match exactly what appears on the physical storefront, business license, and other official documents. If a client requests adding keywords to the name, explain the suspension risk and document that you advised against it.
</Warning>
