Reviews are one of the top three local ranking factors and the most powerful trust signal for potential customers. A consistent review acquisition strategy separates high-performing clients from stagnant ones.

## Review Platforms to Monitor

Not all review platforms are equally relevant for every client. The list below covers the platforms Oovi actively monitors. During onboarding, confirm with the Account Manager which platforms are highest priority for the client's industry.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Google Business Profile" icon="star">
    The primary target for all clients. Google reviews have the most direct impact on Local Pack rankings and are the first thing potential customers see in search results. Every review acquisition strategy must prioritize Google above all other platforms.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Yelp" icon="star">
    Important for many consumer-facing industries including restaurants, home services, and health and beauty. Yelp has strict policies against incentivizing reviews — their algorithm actively filters reviews from accounts that appear to have been solicited.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Facebook" icon="thumbs-up">
    Relevant for B2C businesses with an active Facebook presence. Facebook reviews (now called Recommendations) are visible to the reviewer's network and can drive meaningful referral traffic. Prioritize this platform when the client has an engaged FB following.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Industry Platforms" icon="certificate">
    Vertical-specific platforms can be more influential than Google in certain industries. Examples include Healthgrades and Zocdoc (healthcare), Houzz and Angi (home services), Avvo (legal), and TripAdvisor (hospitality). Confirm which platforms matter most for the client's niche during onboarding.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Review Acquisition Process

A successful review program is built around a consistent, low-friction ask at the right moment in the customer journey. Work with the Account Manager and the client to build a process that fits their workflow.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify Priority Platforms">
    Select 2–3 platforms the client should focus their review acquisition efforts on. For most clients, this will be Google plus one industry-specific platform. Spreading efforts across too many platforms dilutes results — it's better to dominate 2 platforms than to have scattered reviews across 6.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a Direct Review Link">
    Generate the client's Google review shortlink from the GBP dashboard (under "Get more reviews"). This link takes customers directly to the review dialog, removing extra steps that cause drop-off. Shorten it using a URL shortener or branded link tool for use in SMS and printed materials.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Develop a Review Request Message">
    Write review request copy using the Oovi Review Request Templates, available in Google Drive under `/Resources/Templates/Review Management/`. Customize the message for the client's tone and the delivery channel (email, SMS, or verbal script for in-person asks). The message should be brief, warm, and specific to the service the customer received.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the Right Touchpoint">
    Identify the moment in the client's workflow when customer satisfaction is highest — this is where the review ask belongs. Common touchpoints include:
    - A post-service follow-up email sent 24–48 hours after job completion
    - An automated SMS sent after an appointment is marked complete
    - A QR code printed on receipts, invoices, or a countertop card that links to the review page

    Work with the client to implement the touchpoint that fits their existing process. The ask should feel natural, not forced.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a Monthly Review Cadence">
    On the 1st of each month, review the number of new reviews received across all monitored platforms. Log the count in the client's Monthly Reporting tab. If new reviews are below the agreed target (typically 3–5 per month for Google), flag this to the Account Manager before the monthly client call so a corrective action can be discussed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Responding to Reviews

Every review — positive or negative — deserves a response. Review responses show potential customers that the business is attentive and professional, and they give you an opportunity to include relevant keywords naturally.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Respond Within 48 Hours">
    Set a recurring reminder to check all review platforms at least every 48 hours. Timely responses demonstrate that the business is engaged and values customer feedback. Google takes responsiveness into account as a profile engagement signal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Responding to Positive Reviews">
    Thank the reviewer by name if they provided one. Reference the specific service they received or the detail they mentioned. Close with an invitation to return or a forward-looking statement ("We look forward to working with you again"). Keep responses genuine and varied — identical responses across reviews look automated and impersonal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Responding to Negative Reviews">
    Acknowledge the customer's concern without being dismissive. Offer a brief, sincere apology for their experience — without admitting fault for something disputed. Provide a direct way to resolve the issue offline (a phone number or email address). Never argue, make excuses, or respond defensively in a public forum. Even if the complaint is unfair, your response is visible to every prospective customer who reads it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use Templates as a Guide, Not a Script">
    The Oovi Review Response Templates (Google Drive: `/Resources/Templates/Review Management/`) provide a framework for common scenarios. Always customize each response — a clearly copy-pasted reply is worse than no reply at all. Personalization is especially important for negative reviews, where the customer needs to feel genuinely heard.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Handling Fake or Inappropriate Reviews

Occasionally, a client will receive a review that is clearly fake, spam, or violates Google's review policies (off-topic, conflict of interest, prohibited content, etc.). Follow this escalation process:

1. Review Google's prohibited content policy to confirm the review qualifies for removal.
2. Log into GBP, navigate to the review, click the three-dot menu, and select "Flag as inappropriate." Select the most accurate policy violation category.
3. Document the review in the client's project notes: include the reviewer name, date posted, review text, and the date flagged.
4. Allow 2 weeks for Google to evaluate the flag. Google's review team does not guarantee removal and does not provide status updates.
5. If the review is not removed within 2 weeks, escalate to the Account Manager. The Account Manager will determine whether to pursue the formal Business Redressal Complaint Form or advise the client to seek legal counsel in cases involving defamation.

<Tip>
  Never offer incentives — discounts, free services, gift cards, or any other reward — in exchange for a review. This violates Google's and Yelp's terms of service. Google can remove incentivized reviews, suppress future reviews from the account, or flag the profile for policy violations. Yelp's algorithm is particularly aggressive at filtering reviews from accounts with any trace of solicitation. The most effective review strategy is simply asking customers at the right moment — no bribery required.
</Tip>
