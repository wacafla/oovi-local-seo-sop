Citations — mentions of a business's Name, Address, and Phone number across the web — are a core local ranking factor. Consistent, widespread citations signal legitimacy to Google and improve Local Pack visibility.

## What Is a Citation?

A citation is any online mention of a business's NAP (Name, Address, Phone number). Citations come in two forms:

- **Structured citations** are formal directory listings on platforms like Yelp, BBB, or Apple Maps — places where businesses are listed in a consistent, structured format.
- **Unstructured citations** are mentions embedded in other content, such as a news article naming a local restaurant or a blog post recommending a service provider. These count too, though they're harder to build systematically.

The most critical rule in citation building: **NAP must be 100% identical across every source.** Even minor variations — "St" vs. "Street," "Suite 100" vs. "Ste. 100," or a missing area code — create inconsistency signals that dilute your citation authority. Always copy NAP directly from the client intake form and never improvise.

## Citation Tiers

Not all directories are equal. Oovi uses a tiered approach to prioritize citation building efficiently. Always complete Tier 1 before moving to Tier 2, and Tier 2 before Tier 3.

<Accordion title="Tier 1 — Core Directories">
  Tier 1 directories are the most authoritative and widely trusted platforms. Every client must have a complete, accurate, and verified listing on every Tier 1 directory before any other citation work begins.

  - **Google Business Profile** — The highest-priority listing. Treated separately under the GBP Optimization SOP.
  - **Apple Maps** — Surfaces in Apple Maps and Siri results. Claim via Apple Business Connect.
  - **Bing Places for Business** — Feeds Bing Maps and Cortana results.
  - **Facebook Business Page** — High authority and widely indexed by Google.
  - **Yelp** — Critical for many B2C industries (restaurants, home services, health and beauty).
  - **Better Business Bureau (BBB)** — High-trust authority signal, especially for service businesses.
  - **Foursquare** — Powers data for dozens of downstream directories and apps.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Tier 2 — Industry-Specific Directories">
  Tier 2 directories are platforms specific to the client's industry. Relevance matters here more than volume — a listing on Healthgrades carries far more weight for a medical practice than a listing on a generic business directory.

  - **Healthgrades, Zocdoc, WebMD** — Healthcare providers
  - **Avvo, FindLaw, Justia** — Legal professionals
  - **Houzz, Angi, HomeAdvisor** — Home services and contractors
  - **TripAdvisor, OpenTable** — Hospitality and restaurants
  - **Thumbtack, Bark** — General service businesses

  Target the 5–10 most relevant directories for the client's industry. Review the Industry Citation List in the shared Google Drive for a pre-researched list by vertical.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Tier 3 — Local Directories">
  Tier 3 directories are geographically specific. They carry less domain authority than Tier 1 and 2 sources, but they reinforce the business's local relevance signal — a factor Google takes seriously for Local Pack rankings.

  - Local Chamber of Commerce member directory
  - City or neighborhood business directories
  - Local newspaper or media websites
  - Local bloggers or community websites with business listings
  - Regional business associations

  Build Tier 3 citations only after Tier 1 and 2 are complete. Prioritize platforms where the client's competitors already have listings.
</Accordion>

## Building New Citations

Follow this process when building citations for a new client or filling gaps identified in an audit.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Export the Citation Audit from BrightLocal">
    Run a Citation Tracker report in BrightLocal using the client's exact NAP. Export the results and review which directories have accurate listings, inaccurate listings, or no listing at all. This becomes your working task list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify Gaps in Tier 1 Directories">
    Cross-reference the BrightLocal export against the full Tier 1 directory list. Flag every directory where the client is missing, unverified, or has an inconsistent NAP. These are your highest-priority submissions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create or Claim Listings Manually">
    For all Tier 1 directories, manually create or claim each listing. Do **not** use automated submission tools (such as Yext or Moz Local) for Tier 1 — these platforms often format addresses differently or fail to complete all available fields. Manual submissions give you full control over the data.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use Exact NAP from the Intake Form">
    Copy the business name, address, and phone number directly from the client's onboarding intake form. Do not abbreviate, reformat, or correct perceived errors without first confirming with the Account Manager. Consistency is more important than stylistic preference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete All Available Profile Fields">
    Wherever the platform allows it, add photos, a business description, categories, hours of operation, and a website URL. A complete profile performs better than a bare-minimum NAP-only listing and reduces the chance of the directory auto-populating incorrect information.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log Each Submission in the Citation Tracker">
    Record every submission in the client's Citation Tracker spreadsheet in Google Drive. Include: the directory name, the listing URL (or "pending" if not yet live), the date submitted, and the login credentials used (reference the password manager entry — do not log actual passwords in the spreadsheet).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify Listings After 2–3 Weeks">
    Return to each submission after 2–3 weeks to confirm the listing went live and that the NAP is displaying exactly as submitted. Some directories modify submissions during a review process. Correct any discrepancies immediately and note the outcome in the tracker.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Fixing NAP Inconsistencies

When the BrightLocal audit surfaces incorrect existing listings, correct them using the following approach:

1. Navigate to the directory and search for the business listing.
2. Look for a "Claim this listing," "Is this your business?" or "Update business info" option.
3. Create an account or log in with the credentials stored in the client's password manager entry.
4. Claim ownership of the listing, then update the NAP fields to match the intake form exactly.
5. Submit the correction and note the date in the Citation Tracker.
6. Return in 2–3 weeks to verify the correction is live.

If a directory does not offer a self-service correction option, contact their support team directly. Document all correspondence in the client's project notes in case follow-up is needed.

<Note>
  Aim to build 10–15 new quality citations in the first 30 days of a campaign. Do not attempt to build hundreds of citations at once — low-quality, spammy directory submissions can actually harm trust signals. Quality and consistency matter far more than raw volume. A smaller number of well-maintained, accurate citations on reputable platforms will always outperform a large number of hastily built, inconsistent ones.
</Note>
