Every piece of content we produce — from service pages to GBP posts to review responses — represents the client's brand. Writing to a consistent standard ensures quality output and better local SEO performance.

## Tone of Voice

The default Oovi writing style is clear, helpful, and professional. Content should sound like a knowledgeable local expert — someone who genuinely understands the community and the service — not a faceless corporation pushing a product. Avoid jargon-heavy language, overly formal phrasing, and vague filler sentences that don't say anything useful.

Before writing any content for a new client, review their intake form. Pay attention to the brand voice section: some clients want warm and friendly, others want authoritative and technical. Match that tone throughout every piece you write for them. When in doubt, err on the side of clear and conversational — it tends to perform better in local search and resonates with real people.

## Content Types and Length Guidelines

Use the guidelines below for each content type. These are minimums and frameworks, not rigid templates — always adapt to the client's specific business and market.

<Accordion title="Service Pages">
  Service pages are the backbone of most local SEO strategies. Write a minimum of **500 words** per page and cover all of the following:

  - **What the service is** — describe it clearly without assuming prior knowledge
  - **Who it's for** — identify the target customer and their problem
  - **Benefits over competitors** — what makes this business the better choice
  - **Service area** — name the city and relevant neighborhoods explicitly
  - **Call-to-action** — close with a clear next step (call, book, request a quote)

  Target **1–2 primary local keywords** and weave them in naturally. The keyword should appear in the H1, the opening paragraph, and at least one H2 — but only where it reads naturally. Do not force it into every sentence.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Location Pages">
  Location pages target searchers in a specific city or neighborhood. Write a minimum of **400 words of unique content** per page.

  Never duplicate a service page and simply swap out the city name — Google recognizes thin, templated content and it will not rank. Every location page must include:

  - Local landmarks, neighborhoods, or area-specific references that make the content genuinely tied to that location
  - Unique phrasing and structure distinct from every other location page in the same campaign
  - At least one mention of a specific local detail (a street, district, or community reference) that a local reader would recognize as authentic

  If you struggle to write unique content for a location, do additional research: read local news, check neighborhood names on Google Maps, or ask the client what areas they serve most frequently.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Blog Posts">
  Blog posts should target **informational queries** — questions people ask before they're ready to hire ("how to find the best [service] in [city]", "what does [service] cost in [city]"). Aim for **600–1,000 words**.

  Structure each post with a clear introduction, 3–5 body sections with descriptive H2s, and a conclusion that ties back to the client's services. Always include **internal links to relevant service pages** — this passes authority and guides the reader toward conversion.

  Avoid writing purely promotional blog posts. The format works best when it genuinely answers a question the target audience is asking.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="GBP Posts">
  Google Business Profile posts are short, action-oriented updates. Write **150–300 words** and always include:

  - A clear **call-to-action** in every post ("Call today", "Book online", "Learn more", "Request a free quote")
  - An **image** — never publish a GBP post without one. Use a high-quality image relevant to the post topic.

  Keep the writing punchy and direct. GBP posts appear in local search results and on the business panel, so the first sentence needs to grab attention immediately. Highlight a promotion, seasonal service, recent project, or useful tip — not a generic "we're open for business" message.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Review Responses">
  Review responses should be **30–75 words**, personalized, and never templated. Two different 5-star reviews should never receive the same response.

  **For positive reviews:** Thank the reviewer by name (if provided), reference the specific service they mentioned, and invite them to return or recommend the business to others.

  **For negative reviews:** Acknowledge the experience, express genuine empathy, and move the conversation offline — provide a direct phone number or email. Never argue, deflect blame, or make excuses in a public response.
</Accordion>

## Keyword Usage Rules

Keyword placement should feel natural to a human reader. Follow these rules on every piece of content:

- Use the primary keyword in the **H1, title tag, meta description, and first paragraph**
- Use secondary keywords and location variants naturally in **H2s and body content** — don't force them into every heading
- **Never keyword-stuff** — if reading a sentence feels awkward or repetitive, rewrite it. One natural mention beats three forced ones every time.
- Include the **city or neighborhood name at least 2–3 times** per page on service and location pages — not as a keyword pattern, but as a genuine part of describing where the business operates

## Content Quality Checklist

Run every piece of content through these steps before it moves to the approval stage.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check Readability">
    Run the content through the Hemingway App. Aim for **Grade 8 or lower** on service pages. Shorten long sentences, cut passive voice, and simplify unnecessarily complex word choices.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check for Duplicate Content">
    Run the content through **Copyscape or Grammarly's plagiarism checker**. Any match above 10% requires revision before the content moves forward.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify Accuracy">
    Confirm all facts, business details, service descriptions, phone numbers, addresses, and hours are accurate. Cross-reference the client's intake form and their live website.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm Internal Links">
    Click every internal link to verify it leads to a live, correct page. A broken or incorrect internal link reflects poorly on the content and hurts SEO.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Proofread">
    Do a final read-through for grammar, spelling, and punctuation. Run it through Grammarly. Then read it again — ideally out loud — to catch anything Grammarly misses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get Account Manager Approval">
    Submit the content for Account Manager review before publishing to any client website or GBP. Log the approval in ClickUp on the relevant task.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Never use AI-generated content without thorough human editing. Raw AI output is generic and often inaccurate for local details — it may hallucinate business information, use incorrect service area names, or produce content that sounds nothing like the client's brand. Use AI as a starting point or a drafting aid, never as a final product.
</Note>
