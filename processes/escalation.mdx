Problems happen. What separates a great agency from an average one is how quickly and professionally you respond. Every team member has the authority — and the responsibility — to flag an issue the moment they spot it. Silence does not make a problem smaller. Follow this escalation process whenever you encounter an issue that's outside your authority or expertise to resolve alone.

## When to Escalate

If any of the following situations arise, escalate immediately rather than attempting to resolve them independently. Acting without guidance on these issues can make things significantly worse.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="GBP Suspension" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    Escalate to the Account Manager immediately. Do not attempt to submit a reinstatement request, make GBP edits, or contact Google support without manager oversight. Every action taken on a suspended profile is documented and can affect reinstatement outcomes.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Client Complaint" icon="face-angry">
    Any client expressing dissatisfaction — regardless of whether you believe the complaint is valid — escalates to the Account Manager within the same business day. Do not respond to the client directly before the Account Manager is aware of the situation.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Significant Ranking Drop" icon="arrow-down">
    A drop of 5 or more positions for multiple primary keywords requires investigation beyond routine monitoring. Escalate to the SEO Lead as soon as you identify the pattern. Pull data from GSC and BrightLocal before escalating so the Lead can begin triage immediately.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Missed Deadline" icon="clock">
    If you realize you will miss a client deliverable deadline, flag it to the Account Manager at least **48 hours in advance** — never after the fact. Early notice allows the team to reset expectations with the client professionally. Discovering a missed deadline after it's already late is a preventable failure.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Potential Google Penalty" icon="shield-exclamation">
    Any manual action notification appearing in Google Search Console escalates to the SEO Lead immediately. Do not attempt to file a reconsideration request or make site changes without the Lead's involvement. Log the notification with a screenshot in ClickUp under the client's project.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Negative Media or PR" icon="newspaper">
    Any negative press coverage, viral social media complaint, or public controversy involving a client escalates to the Operations Manager. This includes review bombing campaigns, BBB complaints that gain public attention, or news coverage of a client-related incident.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Escalation Steps

When you identify an issue that requires escalation, follow these steps in order. Skipping steps — especially documentation — creates confusion and delays resolution.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Gather the Facts First">
    Before escalating, collect all relevant information: screenshots, dates, what changed, what the current state is, and any actions already taken. Escalating with "something looks wrong" wastes time. Escalating with "GSC shows a manual action dated [date], here's the screenshot, and rankings for [keywords] dropped [X] positions starting [date]" enables immediate action.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Determine the Severity Level">
    Refer to the Severity Levels section below to classify the issue as P1 (Critical), P2 (High), or P3 (Medium). Severity determines who you notify and how fast you need to act. When in doubt, escalate at the higher severity — it's always better to over-communicate than to under-react.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Notify the Right Person Through the Right Channel">
    Use **Slack DM** for P2 and P3 issues. Use **phone** for P1 issues — do not rely on Slack or email for critical escalations where time is a factor. Confirm receipt of your message; if you don't hear back within 30 minutes on a P1, escalate up the chain.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document in ClickUp">
    Log the issue in ClickUp under the client's project. Add the tag **[ESCALATION]** to the task and include all gathered data, the severity level, who was notified, and the time of notification. This creates an audit trail and keeps the full team informed without requiring repeated verbal updates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow Up Daily Until Resolved">
    Escalation is not "fire and forget." You are responsible for following up on the issue daily until it is fully resolved and the ClickUp task is closed. If the assigned owner has not provided an update within the expected timeframe, prompt them via Slack and log the follow-up in ClickUp.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Severity Levels

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="P1 — Critical">
    **Definition:** An issue that is actively harming the client's visibility, reputation, or business right now — or has the potential to within hours.

    **Examples:**
    - GBP suspension
    - Google manual penalty (manual action in Search Console)
    - Client website completely down or returning a 5xx error

    **Response:**
    - Notify the **Account Manager and Operations Manager immediately by phone**
    - Do not wait for a Slack response — call
    - Begin triage within **1 hour** of identifying the issue
    - Log in ClickUp with the [ESCALATION] tag and P1 severity noted in the task description
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="P2 — High">
    **Definition:** An issue that is causing or is likely to cause significant client dissatisfaction, measurable performance damage, or a broken deliverable commitment.

    **Examples:**
    - Client threatening to cancel or expressing serious dissatisfaction
    - Major ranking drop (5+ positions across multiple primary keywords)
    - Missed client deliverable deadline

    **Response:**
    - Notify the **Account Manager same-day via Slack DM**
    - Begin working toward resolution within **24 hours**
    - Log in ClickUp with the [ESCALATION] tag
    - Follow up daily until resolved
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="P3 — Medium">
    **Definition:** An issue that needs attention but is not immediately disrupting client performance or team operations.

    **Examples:**
    - A client question that has exceeded the SLA response window
    - A minor data discrepancy in a report that hasn't yet been sent
    - A tool access issue preventing a team member from completing a task

    **Response:**
    - Log in ClickUp and notify the relevant team member via **Slack DM**
    - Resolve within **2 business days**
    - No phone call required unless the situation escalates
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Post-Resolution

<Note>
  After every P1 or P2 issue is resolved, the Account Manager should send the client a brief written summary covering: what happened, what was done to fix it, and what steps are being taken to prevent it from happening again. This should be clear, factual, and free of blame language. Additionally, log a post-mortem note in the client's ClickUp project documenting the root cause, the resolution, and any process changes being made as a result. Post-mortems are how we get better — treat them as a learning opportunity, not a blame exercise.
</Note>
