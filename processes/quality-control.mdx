Quality control is not optional — it's the last line of defense before a mistake reaches a client. Every deliverable must pass a QC check before it goes live or is sent out. A single error that reaches a client damages trust in a way that takes far longer to repair than the few minutes QC actually takes.

## General QC Principles

Before jumping into deliverable-specific checklists, hold these principles as non-negotiable standards across all work:

- **The person who creates a deliverable should NOT be the only one to QC it.** Fresh eyes catch errors that familiarity masks. Whenever possible, have a peer review your work before it goes to the Account Manager.
- **If you're uncertain about a decision, ask before sending — don't guess.** A quick Slack message takes 30 seconds. Fixing a published mistake can take hours and damage client confidence.
- **Log QC sign-offs in ClickUp on the relevant task.** Every deliverable that passes QC should have a documented record. This protects you, protects the team, and creates an audit trail if questions arise later.

## QC by Deliverable Type

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Content">
    Run through every item on this checklist before submitting content for Account Manager approval:

    - **Keyword placement** — Primary keyword appears in the H1, title tag, meta description, and first paragraph
    - **Word count** — Meets the minimum threshold for the content type (see Content Guidelines)
    - **Readability** — Hemingway App score is Grade 8 or lower for service and location pages
    - **No duplicate content** — Copyscape or Grammarly plagiarism check passes (under 10% match)
    - **Accurate business details** — All names, addresses, phone numbers, hours, and service descriptions match the intake form
    - **Internal links are functional** — Click every link to confirm it leads to a live, correct page
    - **Proofread** — Grammarly has been run and a manual read-through has been completed
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="GBP Changes">
    GBP edits — especially to core fields — can trigger re-verification requests or introduce errors that affect local rankings. Before publishing any change:

    - **Screenshot the current state** of the GBP field before making any edit. Store the screenshot in the client's Google Drive folder.
    - **Verify the change is correct** — cross-reference the client's intake form, official website, or written approval from the client
    - **Check that the business name matches official records exactly** — no added keywords, no punctuation differences, no abbreviation inconsistencies
    - For major changes (business name, primary category, address), confirm written client approval is documented in ClickUp before proceeding
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Citations">
    Citation accuracy is critical — a single NAP inconsistency across dozens of directories compounds into a significant local SEO issue. For every citation built or updated:

    - **Verify NAP matches the master NAP** from the client's intake form exactly — including capitalization, punctuation, suite number format, and phone number format
    - **Confirm the listing went live** — visit the live URL and verify the information is displaying correctly, not stuck in a pending or draft state
    - **Check for duplicate listings** on the same platform — if a duplicate exists, flag it for suppression or removal before moving on
    - Log the citation URL and date in the Citation Tracker in the client's Google Drive folder
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Monthly Reports">
    Reports are one of the most visible deliverables we produce. A report with wrong data, wrong dates, or placeholder text is one of the fastest ways to erode client trust. Before sending any monthly report:

    - **Correct date range** — confirm all data is pulled for the correct reporting month
    - **All data is from the right client** — double-check that GA4, GSC, BrightLocal, and any other data sources are filtered to the correct client property
    - **"Work Completed" section matches ClickUp tasks** — verify every item listed as completed was actually delivered and logged
    - **No placeholder text** — search the document for any leftover template text such as "[CLIENT NAME]", "[INSERT DATA]", or similar
    - **Account Manager has reviewed and approved** — log approval in ClickUp before the report is sent to the client
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Outreach / Link Building">
    A link that doesn't exist, or that points to the wrong page, is wasted effort. Before logging any link as complete:

    - **Verify the link is live** — visit the referring page and confirm the link is visible and clickable
    - **Confirm the link points to the correct page** — hover over the anchor and verify the destination URL
    - **Confirm anchor text is appropriate** — it should be natural, relevant, and aligned with the link building strategy for that client
    - **Log the link in the Link Tracker** — include the referring domain, URL, anchor text, target page, and date acquired
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

<Warning>
  These are the most frequent QC failures across agency work. Review this list before submitting any deliverable:

  - **Sending a report with data from the wrong client** — always confirm data source properties before exporting or copying data into a report
  - **Making GBP edits without client approval for major changes** — name, primary category, and address changes require explicit written approval before you touch them
  - **Building citations with outdated NAP information** — always pull NAP from the intake form, not the client's existing website, which may itself have errors
  - **Publishing content before the Account Manager has reviewed and approved** — no content goes live without sign-off, regardless of how confident you are in the quality
</Warning>

<Tip>
  Build a habit of checking your work against the source of truth — the client's intake form — before sending any deliverable. The intake form is the single most reliable reference for NAP, service details, and client preferences. When something looks off, that's the first place to check.
</Tip>
