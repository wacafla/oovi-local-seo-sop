The kickoff call is the client's first real experience of working with your team. Run it confidently and with a clear structure — it sets the tone for the entire engagement. A well-run kickoff call does three things: it makes the client feel heard, demonstrates that your team has done its homework, and gives everyone a shared understanding of what happens next. Forty-five minutes is enough time to do all three if you come prepared.

## Before the Call

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the Intake Form and Audit">
    Read through the completed intake form and audit findings in full before the call. You should be able to speak to the client's goals, their current gaps, and your top recommendations without referring to notes mid-conversation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the Call Agenda">
    Create a one-page call agenda using the Oovi Kickoff Agenda template in Google Drive. Customize it with the client's name, their top 3 audit findings, and the specific priorities you plan to address in the first 30 days.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify the Top 3 Audit Findings">
    Select the three most important findings from the audit to lead with — prioritize items that are either easy wins or significant blockers. These should anchor the middle section of the call.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the Calendar Invite">
    Send a calendar invite at least 24 hours before the scheduled call. Include a Zoom or Google Meet link, the client's name in the event title, and a brief note on what to expect during the call.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test Your Setup">
    If you plan to screen share the audit or walk through any visuals, test your screen share and audio before the call. Technical issues in the first five minutes undermine confidence.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Call Agenda Structure

Use this timing as your guide. It's designed to keep the call focused and finish on time — clients notice and appreciate that.

| Time | Segment |
|------|---------|
| 0:00 – 0:05 | Introductions and welcome — set a friendly, confident tone |
| 0:05 – 0:15 | Review client goals and success metrics — confirm what matters most to them |
| 0:15 – 0:30 | Walk through audit findings and priorities — lead with the top 3 findings |
| 0:30 – 0:40 | Explain the first 30 days of the campaign — what will happen and when |
| 0:40 – 0:45 | Q&A and next steps — confirm action items on both sides |

## Key Points to Cover

<Accordion title="Communication Cadence">
  Let the client know exactly how and when they'll hear from you. Explain that they'll receive a monthly performance report and that you'll reach out proactively if anything significant changes — they don't need to chase you for updates.

  During this part of the call, confirm:
  - Their preferred contact method (email, phone, text)
  - The best time of day or week to reach them
  - Who on their end should be copied on reports and major updates
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Reporting & KPIs">
  Walk the client through the report template so there are no surprises when the first report arrives. Align on exactly which metrics you'll track — typically local keyword rankings, GBP views and actions, website traffic from local search, and lead volume.

  If there are KPIs they mentioned in the intake form that aren't in the default report, note them and confirm you'll incorporate them before the first reporting cycle.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Access & Collaboration">
  Confirm that all necessary access has been granted (GBP, Google Analytics, Search Console, website CMS). If anything is still missing, assign a specific follow-up action and deadline before ending the call.

  Also explain what you'll need from them going forward — approvals for new content or service page updates, participation in a review request campaign, and prompt responses if you need clarification on business details.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Timeline Expectations">
  Set honest, realistic expectations about how long local SEO takes. Use clear, consistent language:

  - **30–60 days** — early wins are possible (GBP improvements, citation fixes, quick on-page changes), but ranking movement may be limited
  - **90+ days** — this is when more meaningful ranking improvements typically begin to show
  - **6 months** — a fuller picture of campaign performance and compounding results

  Clients who understand the timeline are more likely to stay engaged and less likely to panic at the 30-day mark.
</Accordion>

## After the Call

<Steps>
  <Step title="Send a Follow-Up Email">
    Within 2 hours of the call ending, send a follow-up email using the Oovi Post-Kickoff Email Template. The email should summarize the key discussion points, confirm next steps, and list any outstanding items the client needs to complete.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log Call Notes in ClickUp">
    Add your call notes to the client's project in ClickUp immediately after sending the follow-up email. Include any commitments made, open questions, and the client's stated priorities in their own words.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign First-Week Tasks">
    Create and assign the first week's tasks to the SEO Specialist in ClickUp. Tasks should map directly to the priorities discussed on the call — not a generic checklist.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update Project Status">
    Change the client's project status in ClickUp from "Onboarding" to **"Active"**. This signals to the whole team that the campaign is live and work has begun.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Record the kickoff call with the client's permission. Save the recording in the client's Google Drive folder. Over time, your library of kickoff call recordings becomes one of the most valuable training resources for onboarding new Account Managers — real calls are far more instructive than any script.
</Tip>
