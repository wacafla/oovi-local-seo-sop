The initial audit is the foundation of every campaign. It reveals quick wins, identifies critical gaps, and informs the 90-day strategy. Complete it within 5 business days of receiving the client intake form — not 5 business days from the contract signing date. The audit cannot be done properly without the intake information, so do not start until the form meets the 80% completeness threshold.

## Audit Components

<Steps>
  <Step title="GBP Audit">
    Review the client's Google Business Profile for completeness and accuracy. Check each of the following:

    - **NAP consistency** — name, address, and phone number match exactly what's on the website
    - **Primary category** — confirm it's the most accurate and high-traffic option for their business type
    - **Secondary categories** — verify additional relevant categories are selected
    - **Hours of operation** — confirm regular hours are set and special/holiday hours are up to date
    - **Photos** — a minimum of 10 photos should be present; note the quality and recency
    - **Services / products** — all core offerings should be listed with descriptions
    - **Posts history** — check the recency and frequency of GBP posts
    - **Q&A** — flag any unanswered questions or inaccurate answers
    - **Reviews summary** — note overall rating, total review count, and whether responses are being posted
  </Step>
  <Step title="Citation Audit">
    Run a citation audit in BrightLocal and document the results:

    - Total number of citations found across the web
    - Number and type of NAP inconsistencies (wrong address, old phone number, name variations, etc.)
    - Missing top-tier directories — at minimum, check: Yelp, BBB, Apple Maps, Bing Places, and Facebook
    - Any duplicate listings that need to be suppressed or merged
  </Step>
  <Step title="On-Page Audit">
    Review the homepage and the client's top service pages for local SEO fundamentals:

    - **Title tags** — should include the target city and primary keyword
    - **Meta descriptions** — unique, keyword-relevant, and within character limits
    - **H1 / H2 structure** — logical heading hierarchy with local terms where appropriate
    - **Local schema markup** — check for LocalBusiness schema (or appropriate subtype) using Google's Rich Results Test
    - **Page speed** — run through Google PageSpeed Insights and note Core Web Vitals scores
    - **Mobile-friendliness** — confirm the site renders correctly on mobile devices
  </Step>
  <Step title="Backlink Audit">
    Pull the client's backlink profile in Ahrefs or SEMrush and document:

    - **Domain Rating (DR)** — overall authority benchmark
    - **Total referring domains** — unique domains linking to the site
    - **Toxic or spammy links** — flag any links that may warrant a disavow file
    - **Notable local links** — any local directories, chambers of commerce, or news mentions
  </Step>
  <Step title="Competitor Analysis">
    Identify the top 3 local competitors currently ranking for the client's target keywords. For each competitor, compare:

    - **GBP completeness** — how well-optimized is their profile relative to the client's?
    - **Citation volume** — how many citations do they have, and in which directories?
    - **Review count and rating** — total reviews and average star rating
    - **Domain authority** — DR score as a rough benchmark
    - **On-page optimization** — how are they using keywords, schema, and local signals on their site?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keyword Research">
    Use SEMrush or Ahrefs to identify 10–20 target local keywords for the campaign. Prioritize by:

    - **Search volume** — enough monthly searches to make ranking meaningful
    - **Local intent** — keywords that signal the user is looking for a local provider (e.g., "plumber in Austin" vs. "how to fix a leaky faucet")
    - **Ranking difficulty** — balance high-value keywords with realistic opportunities given the client's current authority
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Audit Deliverable

The completed audit is documented in the Oovi Audit Template (Google Sheets) and saved in the client's dedicated Google Drive folder. The spreadsheet includes a summary tab with key findings and a tab for each audit component. Share the folder link with the Account Manager as soon as the audit is complete so they can begin preparing for the kickoff call.

<Tip>
  Highlight the top 3 quick wins in the audit summary tab — these become the first action items after the kickoff call and give the client an early sense of momentum. Good examples: fixing NAP inconsistencies, adding missing GBP categories, or correcting a missing title tag on the homepage.
</Tip>

## Audit Quality Check

<Note>
  The Account Manager reviews the completed audit before the kickoff call. Build in at least 24 hours between submitting the audit and the scheduled call so the Account Manager has time to review the findings and prepare talking points for the client conversation.
</Note>
