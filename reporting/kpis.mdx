Tracking the right KPIs ensures every campaign stays focused on outcomes that matter to the client. Use these definitions when setting expectations during onboarding and when explaining results in monthly reports.

## Primary KPIs

These are the seven metrics Oovi tracks on every local SEO campaign. Each one maps directly to a business outcome — understand the definition, the data source, and the target before you discuss results with a client.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Local Pack Rankings" icon="map-pin">
    **Definition:** The client's position in Google's Local Pack — the map-based results that appear for local search queries. Positions 1 through 3 are the visible slots; anything below that requires the user to expand the results.

    **Data source:** BrightLocal rank tracker, updated weekly.

    **Target:** Enter the top 3 for all primary target keywords within 90 days of campaign launch.

    **Why it matters:** Local Pack placement directly drives calls, direction requests, and website clicks from high-intent local searchers. It is typically the highest-value ranking a local business can achieve.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Organic Search Rankings" icon="magnifying-glass">
    **Definition:** The client's position in standard blue-link search results for target keywords. Organic rankings complement Local Pack visibility and capture users who scroll past the map results.

    **Data source:** BrightLocal or SEMrush, tracked weekly.

    **Target:** First-page rankings (positions 1–10) within 120 days for medium-competition keywords.

    **Why it matters:** Organic rankings build long-term traffic that is not dependent on the Local Pack. For service-area businesses and multi-location clients, organic rankings extend reach beyond the immediate map radius.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="GBP Search Views" icon="eye">
    **Definition:** The number of times the client's Google Business Profile appeared in search results during the reporting period. This includes both direct searches (for the business by name) and discovery searches (for a category or service).

    **Data source:** GBP Insights, exported monthly.

    **Target:** A consistent upward trend month over month. There is no fixed target number — the baseline is set at the start of the campaign and all growth is measured against it.

    **Why it matters:** Rising search views indicate growing local visibility. It is an early signal that your optimization efforts are working, often before rankings or traffic show movement.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="GBP Actions" icon="cursor-arrow-rays">
    **Definition:** The total number of direct actions taken from the client's GBP listing: clicks to the website, requests for directions, and phone calls. These are the clearest conversion signals available from GBP data.

    **Data source:** GBP Insights, exported monthly.

    **Target:** Month-over-month growth. Track each action type separately so you can identify which are improving and which may need attention.

    **Why it matters:** GBP Actions represent the point where a searcher becomes a potential customer. These metrics are the most direct link between your SEO work and real business activity.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Organic Traffic" icon="chart-bar">
    **Definition:** The number of sessions arriving at the client's website from organic search, as measured in Google Analytics. Always segment by the organic channel — do not mix in direct, referral, or paid traffic.

    **Data source:** Google Analytics, filtered to organic channel.

    **Target:** Focus on the trend over time, not single-month snapshots. A consistent upward trend over 90+ days is the goal. Month-to-month fluctuations are normal.

    **Why it matters:** Organic traffic represents sustainable, unpaid website visits from people searching for the client's services. Growth in this metric means the campaign is expanding the client's digital footprint.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Review Count & Average Rating" icon="star">
    **Definition:** The total number of Google reviews on the client's GBP listing and the current average star rating. Track both the cumulative count and the number of new reviews received each month.

    **Data source:** Google Business Profile dashboard, checked monthly.

    **Target:** Net positive reviews every single month. Maintain a minimum 4.0 star average at all times. Flag any drop below 4.0 to the Account Manager immediately.

    **Why it matters:** Reviews influence both Local Pack rankings and consumer trust. A business with more recent, positive reviews consistently outperforms competitors with fewer or older reviews.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Citation Count" icon="building">
    **Definition:** The total number of structured citations — consistent Name, Address, Phone (NAP) listings on directories, data aggregators, and local platforms — tracked and verified in BrightLocal.

    **Data source:** BrightLocal citation tracker.

    **Target:** 100 or more quality citations within the first six months of the campaign. Quality means accurate NAP data on reputable, indexed platforms — not low-authority spam directories.

    **Why it matters:** Citation consistency and volume are foundational local SEO signals. They reinforce the business's location data for Google and help establish authority in the local market.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Setting Client Benchmarks

Before any optimization work begins, establish a baseline for every KPI above. Document the starting values at month zero — before the first GBP post, before the first citation is built, before any on-page changes are made.

All future reporting compares against those baselines. This gives you an objective measure of progress and protects you from situations where a client claims "nothing has improved" when the data shows otherwise. Store month-zero baselines in the client's folder in Google Drive and reference them in every quarterly strategy call.

## Interpreting Results

Not every KPI will move in the first 30 to 60 days, and that is normal. Set this expectation with every new client during onboarding.

<Info>
  Local SEO results are not linear. Rankings and traffic may fluctuate in the first 60 days as Google processes new signals and re-evaluates the business's relevance and authority. A temporary dip after new work is done does not mean the strategy is failing. Consistent upward trends over 90 or more days are the true indicator of progress — not any single month's numbers.
</Info>

When presenting KPI data to clients, always show the trend over time rather than a single data point. A chart showing three to six months of GBP search views is far more compelling than a single month's number, even if that number is strong.

<Tip>
  Focus client conversations on business outcomes — calls received, foot traffic, form submissions, and leads generated — rather than pure ranking positions. Rankings are a means to an end. A client who understands that their GBP Actions doubled because of your work is far more satisfied than a client who only knows they moved from position 4 to position 2.
</Tip>
