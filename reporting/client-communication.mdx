Clear, proactive communication is one of the most important factors in client retention. Clients who feel informed and heard stay longer, even when results are slow. Every touchpoint — whether a monthly report, a quick Slack-style update, or a difficult phone call — is an opportunity to reinforce that you are on top of their campaign and working in their best interest.

## Response Time Standards

Every Oovi team member is responsible for meeting these response time standards. If you are out of office or unable to respond within the required window, notify the Account Manager so coverage can be arranged before the deadline passes.

- **Email:** Respond within one business day. If the issue requires research before you can give a complete answer, send a brief acknowledgment within the same day and set an expectation for when you will follow up with a full response.
- **Phone and voicemail:** Return calls received before 3:00 PM on the same business day. Return calls received after 3:00 PM by the following morning. Never let a voicemail go unreturned for more than 24 hours.
- **Urgent issues — ranking drops, GBP suspensions, or any situation affecting the client's live presence:** Respond within two hours of becoming aware of the issue. Escalate immediately to the Account Manager and begin investigating the cause. Do not wait until you have a solution to reach out — contact the client as soon as you know there is a problem.

## Regular Communication Cadence

Staying in regular contact keeps clients engaged and reduces the likelihood of surprise cancellations. Follow this cadence for every active client.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Monthly Report" icon="file-text">
    Sent by the 3rd of each month. Always include a short personal note summarizing the highlight of the month — never send the report as a bare attachment or link without context. The note does not need to be long; two to three sentences is enough.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Quarterly Strategy Call" icon="phone">
    A 30-minute call scheduled at the end of each quarter. Use this time to review cumulative progress, discuss any strategy adjustments for the next quarter, and reinforce the client relationship. These calls significantly reduce churn.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Ad Hoc Updates" icon="bell">
    Proactively message the client when something notable happens — a significant ranking jump, a GBP issue that needs their attention, or an influx of new reviews. Do not wait for the monthly report to share good news or flag a problem.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Campaign Milestones" icon="flag">
    Send a brief, celebratory note when the client hits a meaningful milestone: their first Page 1 ranking, reaching 50 Google reviews, entering the Local Pack for a primary keyword. These moments reinforce the value of the campaign.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Professional Tone Guidelines

How you say something is just as important as what you say. Use these guidelines to navigate the most common — and most difficult — client communication scenarios.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Delivering Bad News" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    Be direct and solution-oriented from the first sentence. Do not bury the issue at the end of a long email or soften it with so much positive framing that the client misses the point.

    Follow this structure:
    1. Acknowledge the issue clearly and without defensiveness
    2. Explain the most likely cause in plain language
    3. Present your specific plan to address it and the expected timeline
    4. Invite the client to ask questions or schedule a call if they want to discuss it

    Never hide problems, delay reporting them, or go silent hoping things will resolve on their own. Clients always find out, and discovering that you knew and didn't tell them is far more damaging to the relationship than the original issue.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Explaining Slow Results" icon="clock">
    Reference the timeline that was agreed upon at the start of the campaign. Remind the client that local SEO is a long-term investment and that the targets discussed during onboarding are measured over months, not weeks.

    When rankings have not yet moved significantly, point to leading indicators that are trending in the right direction: GBP search views increasing, citation count growing, new reviews coming in. These are legitimate signals of progress even before rankings shift.

    Avoid vague reassurances like "things are moving in the right direction." Back every statement with a specific data point from the most recent report.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Responding to Frustrated Clients" icon="face-frown">
    Acknowledge their frustration before you say anything else. Skipping this step and jumping straight to data or explanations makes clients feel dismissed and typically escalates the situation.

    Use language like: *"I hear you — I understand this is frustrating, and I want to make sure we address your concerns directly."*

    Do not get defensive about the work that has been done. Offer a 15-minute call to walk through the data together. Talking through the numbers live, with you guiding the conversation, is almost always more effective than a long email exchange. Log the interaction in ClickUp and notify the Account Manager.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What NOT to Promise

No matter how confident you are in the strategy or how strong early results look, there are specific commitments you must never make to a client.

<Warning>
  Never guarantee specific ranking positions or promise that results will arrive by a specific date. SEO outcomes depend on competition levels, algorithm updates, the client's website, their review volume, and many other factors outside your direct control.

  What you can — and should — promise:
  - Consistent execution of best-practice local SEO strategies
  - Transparent, on-time reporting every month
  - Proactive communication when issues arise
  - Adherence to the scope of work outlined in the contract

  If a client asks for a ranking guarantee, explain why reputable agencies do not offer them and redirect the conversation to the timeline and targets documented in their onboarding materials.
</Warning>
