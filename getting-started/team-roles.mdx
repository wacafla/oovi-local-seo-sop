Knowing who owns what is the difference between a campaign that runs smoothly and one that falls through the cracks. This page defines every role at Oovi Local SEO — what each person is responsible for, what they own, and how their work connects to the rest of the team. Whether you're figuring out where your own responsibilities begin and end, or you need to know who to loop in on a specific task, this is your reference.

## Roles & Responsibilities

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Account Manager" icon="briefcase">
    Owns the client relationship from day one. Leads kickoff calls, sets expectations, handles all client-facing communication, and reviews monthly reports before they're sent. The AM is the client's primary point of contact and the internal escalation point for anything affecting client satisfaction.
  </Card>
  <Card title="SEO Specialist" icon="magnifying-glass">
    Executes the technical and on-page SEO strategy for assigned accounts. Conducts site audits, manages and optimizes Google Business Profiles, builds and cleans up citations, and implements on-page recommendations. The SEO Specialist owns the day-to-day campaign execution.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Content Writer" icon="pen">
    Creates SEO-optimized content for client websites, writes Google Business Profile posts on a regular schedule, and drafts review request messaging for clients. All content must align with the client's brand voice and the keyword strategy set by the SEO Specialist.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Link Building Specialist" icon="link">
    Manages outbound link prospecting and outreach campaigns. Identifies relevant, high-quality link opportunities, conducts outreach, tracks all placements in the shared link-building log, and reports on domain authority gains monthly.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reporting Analyst" icon="chart-bar">
    Compiles monthly performance reports for all active client accounts. Tracks KPIs across Google Analytics, Google Search Console, and BrightLocal. Flags significant ranking changes or traffic anomalies to the relevant Account Manager ahead of report delivery.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Operations Manager" icon="gear">
    Oversees all SOPs and ensures this documentation stays current. Manages new hire onboarding, controls tool access and subscription seats, and is the internal point of contact for process questions, system issues, and team-wide workflow changes.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Collaboration Norms

A lean agency works because everyone communicates proactively and uses shared systems consistently. These norms aren't optional — they're what keep campaigns from stalling and clients from being left in the dark.

- **All task assignments go through the project management tool.** Do not delegate, accept, or track work over Slack or email. If it's not in ClickUp (or Asana), it doesn't officially exist.
- **Communicate blockers the same day you identify them.** If something is preventing you from completing a deliverable, tell your manager or the relevant teammate immediately — not on the due date.
- **Weekly team sync happens every Monday.** Attendance is expected. This is where we align on client priorities, flag upcoming deadlines, and surface any account concerns before they become problems.
- **Client-facing communication is owned by the Account Manager.** If you're not the AM on an account, do not contact the client directly unless explicitly asked to.
- **Document everything.** Meeting notes, audit findings, outreach responses, ranking changes — if it's relevant to a client account, it goes in the shared Google Drive folder for that client.
- **Tag, don't assume.** If a task or question is time-sensitive, tag the relevant person directly in the project management tool rather than assuming they'll see a general comment.

<Note>
  At a small agency, one person may hold multiple roles. That's normal and expected. Always refer to these role definitions to understand what tasks belong to each function — it keeps accountability clear even when the same person is wearing more than one hat.
</Note>
